Privacy Policy for the Knees First app

Last updated: November 6, 2026

Summary

  • The app has no sign-up and no account. It shows no ads and does not track you.
  • The apps you choose to block, your Screen Time and lock data and your day streak stay only on your phone. They are never sent to us or to anyone else (for backups, see section 2).
  • The Bible verses and prayers are built into the app. The app does not download content.
  • RevenueCat confirms your subscription. It receives a random code, your purchase details and technical information about your device (section 3.2).
  • Two things are optional and happen only if you agree, each with its own choice:
    • usage analytics with PostHog, on servers in the European Union, without your name, email or an account, linked to a random code (section 3.3);
    • notices from Knees First sent over the internet (section 3.4).

    If you say no, the app works the same. You can change your mind at any time in Settings > Privacy and notices.

  • The daily prayer reminder is created on your phone and sends nothing (section 2).
  • Because Knees First is a Christian app, your subscription record, usage data and notice record may indicate your faith. We treat this data as sensitive (section 4).
  • We do not sell your personal information and do not use it for advertising by other companies.

How to delete: uninstalling the app deletes this data from the device. On iPhone, an earlier iCloud backup may still contain your streak and settings until you delete it or it is replaced.

3. Purchases, usage analytics and notices

3.1 App Store and Google Play

Payment is handled by the App Store (Apple) or Google Play (Google), under their own privacy policies. The app does not ask for or receive your name, email or card details.

3.2 RevenueCat

We use RevenueCat, Inc. (United States) to confirm purchases and to know whether your subscription is active. RevenueCat acts as our processor: it handles the data only to provide this service, under its data processing agreement. The RevenueCat account the app uses belongs to a project collaborator, and that agreement is made with that account. The collaborator can also see this data, acts on our behalf and uses it only to keep the app working. RevenueCat receives:

  • A random identifier created by the app at installation (it starts with $RCAnonymousID:). It is not linked to your name or email. If you reinstall the app, a new identifier is created.
  • Purchase data: the App Store receipt or the Google Play purchase token and, from them, the product (weekly or annual), the dates of start, renewal, trial, cancellation and refund, the price, the currency and the store country.
  • Technical information: device model and brand, system version, app version, preferred languages, store country and the last time the app connected. On iPhone it also includes the "identifier for vendor" (IDFV), a code Apple creates for the apps of one developer on that device.
  • The IP address of the connection. According to RevenueCat, it is used to estimate the country and then discarded, not stored.

What for: to confirm, restore and keep your subscription, unlock the paid features, prevent fraud and produce aggregate subscription statistics (for example, how many trials become subscriptions).

RevenueCat does not receive the apps you block, Screen Time or app usage data, your streak, your settings or any prayer content.

Where: on servers in the United States (Amazon Web Services), over an encrypted connection.

How long: as long as needed to manage your subscription and meet legal obligations. On request, we delete your RevenueCat record (section 8).

The random identifier is also the "Support ID" and, if you agree, it is used for usage analytics and notices (sections 3.3 and 3.4). The beginning $RCAnonymousID: is only RevenueCat's technical name for this code. With it we can find your records, so we treat this data as personal data.

We require every third party that handles the app's data to give it the same protection as this policy, or equivalent protection.

3.3 Usage analytics (optional): PostHog

This happens only if you agree to share usage data: at the start of the app ("Usage data"), in the update notice or in Settings ("Share usage data"). Without this choice, the app sends no usage events. If you accept only notices, the PostHog tool is used only for the notice record in section 3.4.

Who: PostHog, Inc. (a United States company), acting as our processor. We use PostHog Cloud EU: the data is kept on servers in Frankfurt, Germany.

What is sent:

  • The random code from section 3.2 (the same "Support ID"), to count devices without knowing who you are.
  • Usage events from Knees First:
    • app opens;
    • the steps of the app's start, your answers to permission requests (allowed, denied or later) and whether the lock was ready at the end;
    • prayers started, abandoned and completed from the home screen;
    • subscription screens (when they appear or are closed and the plan chosen), purchases, restores and changes of the subscription state (no subscription, trial, active, ended);
    • your choices about analytics, notices and the daily reminder;
    • notice opens (which notice was opened).
  • App and device characteristics: platform, app language, app version, system and version, device model and type, screen size, system language, approximate time zone (only the time-offset zone, for example the same value for all of US Eastern Time, never the city or state), the installation date, the subscription state, whether you finished the app's start, whether system notifications are allowed, whether you accepted analytics and notices, the version of the choice text you saw and the version of the analytics tool.
  • If you also accept notices: the notice record from section 3.4.

What is never sent:

  • which apps you chose or how many;
  • Screen Time data or data about your use of other apps;
  • when the lock appears, emergency unlocks, or anything that happens on the lock screen;
  • anything while the app is open from the lock: when you open the app from the lock screen or its notification (for example with the pray button), it sends no events until it goes to the background;
  • your day streak;
  • the text of verses or prayers;
  • your name, email, phone number or location;
  • your IP address (PostHog discards it, and we do not use IP-based location);
  • advertising identifiers (IDFA, AAID) or device identifiers (IDFV, ANDROID_ID).

What for: to understand how many people use the app and come back, where the app's start is difficult and how the subscription works, so we can improve the app. If you also accept notices, this data is also used to choose groups that receive a notice (for example by language, or people who have not opened the app for a week), always with at least 20 devices per group. We do not use this data for ads, do not sell it and do not make decisions about you. Usage analytics is not connected to RevenueCat: the two exchange no data.

How long:

  • Events are kept for 1 year. This is the period of PostHog's plan and cannot be shortened.
  • The device record in PostHog (the characteristics above and the notice record from section 3.4) is deleted, with its events, after 13 months without receiving anything from the app, or earlier if you ask for deletion (section 8). While you use the app with notices on, the app renews this record at most once every 30 days, with no new data. We run this cleanup once a month.
  • When you turn analytics off, the app stops sending at once and discards what it had not sent yet. If "Get notices" stays on, the app also removes from the device record the characteristics used only for analytics (app version, subscription state, notification permission and whether you finished the app's start). If notices are off, these characteristics may stay in the record until the 13-month cleanup above, or until you ask for deletion (section 8). Events already sent stay until they are 1 year old, unless you ask for deletion (section 8).

3.4 Notices (optional): Expo, Apple, Google and Cloudflare

This happens only if you agree to receive notices ("Get notices" in Settings) and system notifications are allowed. It is a separate choice from usage analytics.

How it works: the app asks Expo's notification service (650 Industries, Inc., United States) for a notice code (token) for this device. Expo gets this code from Apple (on iPhone) or from Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging, on Android). Together with the random code, the app keeps a notice record in PostHog (section 3.3) with: the token, the approximate time zone, the app language, the platform, the installation date, whether you accepted notices, whether you accepted usage analytics and the version of the choice text you saw. The app sends this record again when something in it changes and, while you use the app, at most once every 30 days, to show that the device is still active. PostHog keeps each sending of the record as a technical event, without usage data, for 1 year. This record exists even if you declined usage analytics; if you never accepted analytics, it contains no usage events. If you accepted usage analytics and later turned it off, the record may also contain technical characteristics recorded while analytics was on.

To send a notice, we use our own panel, which runs on Cloudflare, Inc. (United States) and can be opened only by the controller, with a login. The panel reads this record from PostHog and hands the notice text to Expo, which passes it to Apple or Google and on to your device. The panel's send log is kept in a Cloudflare database in the European Union; the panel may process a send on Cloudflare servers in other countries.

Which notices:

  • App news and messages for everyone who accepted notices.
  • Messages for a group (for example by language, or for people who have not opened the app for a week) go only to people who also accepted usage analytics.
  • At most 1 notice every 7 days and 3 every 30 days, only between 9 a.m. and 8 p.m. in your time zone, and never in the first 48 hours after installation.
  • Notices never mention the apps you block, unlocks or your streak.
  • The controller approves every text before it is sent.

Who receives what:

  • Expo: the token and, for each send, the notice text. According to Expo, the content is kept only until it is delivered to Apple or Google.
  • Apple and Google: the device token and the notice text, to deliver it. On Android, Google's service also uses a Firebase installation identifier. Before you accept notices, the app does not request a token and does not start Firebase.
  • Cloudflare: during a send, the token, time zone, language and random code pass through the panel's memory and are not stored. To respect the frequency limits, the panel keeps a send log for 31 days with codes derived from the random code and the token (encrypted with a secret key), never the real values. In the same way it keeps tokens that stopped working for 180 days.

What for: to send the notices you agreed to receive.

How long: when you turn notices off, the app cancels the token on the device at once and deletes the token from the record in PostHog (if the phone is offline, the next time you open the app). Send logs at Cloudflare are deleted within 31 days (logs of tokens that stopped working within 180 days). The notice record in PostHog follows the 13-month period in section 3.3. Expo, Apple and Google may keep the technical link between the token and the device for longer, under their own rules.

4. Sensitive data and legal bases

Sensitive data: Knees First is a Christian app. So the record that you subscribe or started a trial, your usage data and your notice record may indirectly reveal your religious beliefs. Many privacy laws treat religious beliefs as sensitive data, including Brazil's LGPD, the Philippines' Data Privacy Act, South Africa's POPIA, the data protection laws of Nigeria and Kenya, and several US state laws. We never ask your religion and keep no information about it. We still apply the rules for sensitive data to this data.

Your subscription (section 3.2): we process it because it is necessary for your subscription: to validate the purchase, unlock and restore the subscription, follow renewals, cancellations and refunds, and prevent fraud in purchase validation. Without it, we cannot confirm your purchase or unlock the paid features. We also use these records, only as totals across all subscriptions, to manage the subscription offer. We do not build individual profiles.

Where a privacy law does not allow sensitive data to be processed only because it is needed for a contract (for example, the Philippines), we also rely on your consent. Directly above the purchase button, the subscription screen tells you that RevenueCat, in the United States, processes your purchase record for us and that, because Knees First is a Christian app, this record may indicate your faith, with a link to this policy. You give your consent by subscribing or starting a free trial. You can withdraw it by cancelling the subscription and asking us to delete your RevenueCat record (section 8); without it, we cannot keep your subscription working.

Usage analytics and notices (sections 3.3 and 3.4): only with your specific consent, given separately for each purpose and shown clearly before you choose. You can withdraw each consent at any time, free of charge and without losing any function of the app (section 8). Withdrawal does not affect what was done before it.

The daily reminder (section 2) sends no data and so does not depend on consent.

What we do not do with this data: we do not use it for ads, profiles or decisions about you, and we do not sell it. Apple and Google handle your purchase as independent controllers, under their own policies (section 3.1).

Our processors handle the data under data processing agreements, over encrypted connections. They receive only a random code, never your name or email.

The panel never shows tokens or identifiers, only totals, and stores tokens and identifiers only as codes encrypted with a secret key.

8. Your rights and how to use them

Depending on where you live (section 10), you can ask us to:

  • confirm whether we process your data;
  • give you access to it, or a copy in a portable format;
  • correct it;
  • delete it;
  • tell you who we share it with;
  • withdraw a consent;
  • object to or restrict a processing.

You can also complain to your privacy regulator (section 10).

How to ask: write to mertct111@gmail.com. Because we do not know who you are, include one of these so we can find your record:

  • the "Support ID" (in the app: Settings > About, tap "Copy"; it is the random identifier from section 3.2);
  • your Apple order ID (in the receipt email);
  • your Google Play order number (it starts with "GPA.").

We answer within 15 days.

With the Support ID, we delete the device record in PostHog with all its events. To also delete your record at RevenueCat, we also ask for the order number from your purchase receipt, or we reply to your email asking for a confirmation, so that we do not delete someone else's subscription. Send logs at Cloudflare are deleted automatically within 31 days (180 days for tokens that stopped working). The Support ID is the same code used for usage analytics and notices. Send your Support ID only to our email address, and do not post it publicly.

Withdraw consents: in the app, go to Settings > Privacy and notices and turn off "Share usage data" or "Get notices". Turning analytics off stops sending at once. Turning notices off cancels the token on the phone at once. If the phone is offline, or if the app was opened from the lock screen, PostHog is updated the next time you open the app. The daily reminder is turned off in the same place, under "Daily prayer reminder".

Remove permissions: on iPhone, in Settings > Screen Time. On Android, in the system settings under Usage access and Display over other apps (names may vary by manufacturer). You can also simply uninstall the app.

Cancel the subscription: through the App Store or Google Play. The steps are on the support page. Uninstalling the app does not cancel the subscription.

9. Children

Knees First is made for adults who want to limit their own phone use. In the App Store its age rating is 4+ because the content suits all ages, and in Google Play its target audience is 18 and over. The app is not made for children. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. In some places, such as Texas, the App Store and Google Play ask a parent to approve downloads and purchases on a minor's account.

The choices about usage analytics and notices are made by the person using the app. If you learn that a child under 13 accepted, write to us and we will delete the data.
